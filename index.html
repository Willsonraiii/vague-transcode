What it finds

✗Variable frame ratethe 60→30 killer

The single most common reason a 60fps export gets delivered at 30. Platforms re-time VFR footage. Read straight from the frame timing table, not guessed.

✗Broken HDR tagsthe grey look

Wide-gamut pixels labelled as SDR — the classic CapCut export fault. It's why HDR footage comes out washed out and milky.

✗Transfer damagealready too late

A 4K file that's 8-bit H.264 SDR was re-encoded by WhatsApp or by iOS "Automatic" transfer. That loss happened before you even opened this page.

What nobody else will tell you

Every platform re-encodes every upload. TikTok accepts up to 4K and 120 fps — and our 4K60 test post came back from TikTok as 4K 60 (measured, Sept 2026). The re-encode still happens; the source you feed it decides how good it looks.

Tools claiming "no compression" are describing the file they hand back to you — not what your viewers actually receive. Whether your account is served above 1080p is measurable: post, download it back, and use Did it survive? below.

What you can control: the quality of the source their encoder works from, and whether your frame rate and colour survive the trip. That's this.

Need more than the container fixed?

TikTok delivers 4K uploads as 4K (measured) — there's usually no reason to downscale at all anymore. If your file needs an actual re-encode (10-bit/HDR, or locking a variable frame rate), that can't happen safely in a browser — a canvas is SDR only, and ffmpeg.wasm's libx265 deadlocks on 10-bit source.

Drop your file above and use the Download re-encode script button. It generates a drag-and-drop script for your OS that installs ffmpeg if missing and runs the real 10-bit HLG/x265 encode natively — nothing is uploaded anywhere.
